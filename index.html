This Week's Timetable

Trains start at Ravensgill Junction, run up the dale to Moorend and back. Give each locomotive (and its carriage set) a list of departure times. Untick stations to make a train run through them without stopping, or untick the ends to make a shuttle (it must start and turn back at a loop, ⇄). The line is single track: trains can only pass at the loops at Lowerdale and Fellside (and Brackenby or Highmoor Summit, if you build them), so the signallers will hold one train at a loop until the other has cleared the single line.

Events Calendar

Book galas, festivals and specials for future weekends. Book early: posters, adverts and the enthusiast press need about four weeks to spread the word, and an event booked at short notice draws a smaller crowd. A quarter of the cost is paid as a deposit when you book.

Motive Power

Steam pulls the crowds but needs three crew and costs most to run. Heritage diesels are cheaper and reliable, and the diesel-gala crowd loves them. A diesel multiple unit needs no carriage set and turns round in minutes, but nobody travels specially to ride one.

Carriage Sets

A locomotive needs a rake. Longer sets carry more people but are harder work up the 1 in 60 to Highmoor Summit: check the loco can haul it.

Works & Overhaul

A running repair is quick. A heavy overhaul restores a loco to as-new but takes it out of traffic for weeks: do them in winter.

Volunteers & Upgrades

Like every heritage line, the R&H runs on volunteers. Each operating day needs signallers and station staff, a crew for every train (three for steam, two for a diesel or DMU) and stewards for events. Busy weekends tire them out.

How to Play

You are general manager of the Ravensgill & Highmoor Railway, a preserved branch line in the Pennine dales of the north of England. Run normal services, organise steam and diesel galas, beer and music festivals, 1940s weekends and Santa specials, and grow the line until it's named Heritage Railway of the Year.

  • The laptop: each part of the railway is an app on the desktop. Double-click an icon (or tap it on a touch screen) to open its window; drag windows by the title bar, resize them from the corner, and switch between them on the taskbar. The Start button (the R&H roundel) lists every app.
  • Mail: railtour companies and other heritage railways email asking to hire your locomotives. Accept their offer, decline, or send a counter-offer: ask for more, but push too hard and they'll go elsewhere. A hired-out engine is away for the weeks agreed, and comes back a little more worn.
  • The yard: when you press Run the Weekend you can drive the Class 08 shunter yourself to marshal the first train: the laptop folds away and you're in the cab. Form the train on the Platform road in the order shown, using the headshunt and Sidings A and B. ↑/↓ (or W/S) drive, Space brakes, ←/→ (or A/D, or drag) look around, 1/2/3 set the points, U uncouples the far vehicle. Buffer up gently (under 2 mph) to couple. A clean, quick job lifts reputation and morale; or let the shed staff do it.
  • Each turn is one week. Plan the weekend in the Timetable app, then press Run the Weekend. Choose how many days to run: weekends, four days a week in the summer school holidays, or close for the week. The weekend then plays out live on the Line Map, all its days together in the time of one (change the speed there, or pause it). You can't start the next week until the last train is home, but you can carry on booking events, buying stock and recruiting while it runs. The takings arrive when the weekend ends.
  • Timetabling: every train is a round trip from Ravensgill Junction to Moorend and back, about two hours with turn-rounds. Give each loco a set of departure times. If a train isn't back in time, its next trip leaves late.
  • The printed timetable: the Up and Down tables at the top of the Timetable app are the public timetable, a column for each train. The times are worked out by the signallers' plan, so a train held at a loop shows its real, later time. | means the train runs through without stopping.
  • Shuttles and non-stop trains: each train working has a row of stations it calls at. Untick a station in the middle and the train runs straight through it: quicker, but nobody can get on there. Untick the ends to make a shuttle, say Fellside–Moorend: it can only start and turn back at a passing loop (⇄), runs out empty from the shed in the morning and back at night, and its shorter rides sell cheaper tickets. Each station's passengers wait for a train that calls there; most people join at Ravensgill, where the car park is.
  • Double-heading: press 🚂🚂 Double-head on a diagram to couple a pilot engine on the front. The two engines' loads add together (a 4-coach and a 5-coach engine can take 9 coaches up the bank), so small engines can work long trains and climb faster. The crowd likes the sight, and a double-header counts as two engines for a gala, but it needs two crews, burns both engines' coal and wears both engines, and the train goes at the slower engine's pace.
  • Single line: trains can only pass at the loops (Lowerdale and Fellside, plus Brackenby and Highmoor Summit once built), and only one train may be on each single-line section. The train graph shows where trains are held. Each loop has one road for each direction (Moorend holds two trains), so too many trains with too few loops means trains are held and run late.
  • Passengers arrive through the day (mostly late morning and lunchtime) and take the next train. A gap of more than two hours loses them; a full train turns them away. Steam draws more people than diesel, and both draw more than a DMU.
  • Visiting stock: any event can bring visiting engines (up to 3) and visiting carriages (up to 2) on hire. For no gala at all, book Visiting Engine Running Days: ordinary running days with guests, on as many days as you like.
  • Saving: press 💾 Save / Load for a link containing the whole railway. Copy it, email it to yourself or download it as a file, then open it on any device (the Pi included) to carry on.
  • Events are booked on the calendar, ideally four weeks ahead. Galas need at least three engines of the right type in the timetable: hire guest engines when you book. Beer & music festivals fill the evening trains and the bar. Santa specials are the winter goldmine.
  • Volunteers crew every train. If a day needs more than turn up, you can't run it. Recruit, and keep morale up with a quiet week or a social.
  • Condition falls with every mile. Worn locos fail, and a failure blocks the line, refunds fares and costs reputation. Use the Works, and do heavy overhauls in winter.
  • Seasons: spring and autumn are steady, summer is busy and the school holidays busiest of all; winter is dead apart from Santa specials. Weather matters too: check the forecast.
  • Goal: reach a net worth of £500,000 with a reputation of 110 or more. Finish two weeks in a row overdrawn and the railway closes.

The Ravensgill & Highmoor Railway, its stations and the companies that write to you are fictional; the rolling stock is inspired by real preserved locomotives.
